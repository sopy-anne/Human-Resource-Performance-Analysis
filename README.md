# Human-Resource-Performance-Analysis
![](https://github.com/sopy-anne/Human-Resource-Performance-Analysis/blob/main/images.png)

This HR Analytics report provides a data-driven overview of an organization’s workforce, focusing on key indicators that influence employee performance, retention, satisfaction, and overall organizational welfare.

The analysis explores:
- workforce demographics
- attrition patterns,
- promotion readiness,
- satisfaction trends,
- work-life balance,
- identification of employees with risk factors such as overtime or low satisfaction.

This provides actionable insights that support informed decision-making, improve employee engagement, strengthen retention strategies, and align workforce planning with organizational objectives.

## Data Cleaning and Analysis Process

- Data collected was cleaned and transformed with Microsoft Excel 
- The key analysis involved measures of central tendency to summarise the data and identify the average and patterns of the key variables in the dataset.
- The data was visualised using PowerBI
  
## Key Findings of the analysis 

#### Overview of Total Workforce

- The company has a total of 1470 employees, with 1233 currently active.
- 60% of the employees are male, and 40% are female
- The company is made up of three departments, with Research and Development as the largest, followed by Sales, and a smaller number in the Human Resources department.

### Monthly payroll and distribution among employees  
- The organization incurs $10 million in monthly payroll expenses.
- A significant 51% of the workforce earns at the lower-income level
- The current compensation levels may lead to attrition among top performers, especially those who are underpaid relative to their contribution.

### Employee Attrition

- Attrition rate appears moderate, with a yes Vs No ration suggesting retention is generally stable, but with clear risk factors
- Females in sales and males in R&D show higher attrition occurrences.
- This indicates role-specific and possibly stress-related or workload-related issues.
- Younger, low-income, early-career employees, and employees with long-distance commutes are the most vulnerable to turnover.

### Promotion Readiness

- Several employees with 4+ years of experience in the same role and high performance have lower-than-average monthly income, signalling a salary mismatch with performance and promotion.
- Many employees with advanced education recorded 5+ years since their last promotion, signalling stagnation risk, especially among high-potential staff.

### Satisfaction Levels
 - Majority of the employees are satisfied (882); however, a significant number (290) indicated a poor satisfaction level.
 - There is a slight variation in the satisfaction level of employees based on their marital status.

### Work-Life Balance and Overtime

- Employees with a balanced work-life balance are top performers
- However, a few of the employees are recoreded poor work-life balance.
- 23% of current employees did overtime. 
- Many employees with overtime are high performers with work-life balance, indicating that the burnout does nto influence the employees.

## Recommendations

Based on the identified insights from this analysis, the recommendations include: 

- Improve Retention of At-Risk Employees: Focus on younger employees, low-income earners, and long-distance commuters. Offer relocation support, transport allowances, or hybrid working options.

- Review Compensation & Promotion Policies: Prioritize high-performing employees with 4+ years in the same role for salary review. Introduce transparent promotion pathways and mentorship programs.

## Conclusion

While the performance of the employees are on the average, it is important the the organisation look into promotion and compensation policies to reduce attrition and continue to maintain high retention.
